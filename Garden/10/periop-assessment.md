>[!EXAMPLE] Situation 1
>Kyuti Bato, a 12-year-old, joined their annual school field trip together with friends Bata, Bati, and Batu. On their way home at 9:02 PM, a car crashed into their school bus, causing a mass casualty incident.

1. Kyuti Bato suffered a traumatic brain injury leading to brain herniation, manifesting as respiratory problems, rigid extension of extremities, and dilated unreactive pupils. As a nurse receiving Kyuti Bato, who would you likely refer this case to?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Tere, a pediatrician</li>
	<li>Maja, on-call physician</li>
	<li>Adrinne, a neurosurgeon</li>
	<li>Denver, OR nurse</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>C. **Adrinne**, a neurosurgeon.

2. Kyuti Bato will undergo craniectomy to evacuate the hematoma. According to this case, this is classified under what kind of urgency?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Urgent</li>
	<li>Imperative</li>
	<li>Emergent</li>
	<li>Required</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>C. **Emergent**. Emergent cases are those that cannot be delayed and are immediately life-threatening. Urgent, otherwise known as imperative, can wait for 2 hours or more. Required surgeries can be delayed by 1 week to 1 month.

3. Kyuti Bato is undergoing surgery. His vital signs are BP 100/70, HR 70, RR 13, O₂ sat 99%. If his vital signs turn abnormal and far from baseline, who would report this to the surgeon?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Surgeon</li>
	<li>Anesthesiologist</li>
	<li>Circulating nurse</li>
	<li>Scrub nurse</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>B. **Anesthesiologist**. The anesthesiologist manages vital signs.

4. The WHO Surgical Safety Checklist aims to enhance communication and teamwork within the surgical team. This was initiated before the procedure. As part of the surgical team, who is responsible for initiating this checklist?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Porter</li>
	<li>Circulating nurse</li>
	<li>Scrub nurse</li>
	<li>Surgeon</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>B. **Circulating nurse**. The circulating nurse coordinates the entire surgical team, and initiates the operative checklist. The scrub nurse is responsible for the overall sterility of the sterile field.

5. Two weeks post-op, Kyuti Bato is returned with dizziness, nausea, and blurred vision. A sponge is found at the incision site. With the doctrine of *res ipsa loquitur* and captain-of-the-ship, who is to blame? Select all that apply.
	1. Anesthesiologist
	2. Surgeon
	3. Scrub nurse
	4. Circulating nurse
	5. Porter

<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>2, 3</li>
	<li>2, 4</li>
	<li>1, 2, 3, 4</li>
	<li>1, 2, 3, 4, 5</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **2, 3**. The surgeon is the "captain of the ship" and carries the greatest liability. The scrub nurse is also responsible for counting instruments, and is therefore also responsible for the injury.

>[!EXAMPLE] Situation 2
>Mrs. Pham, a 62-year-old hypertensive patient, is scheduled for CABG. During her preoperative assessment, the nurse checks her vital signs, labs (CBC, urinalysis, blood chemistry), ECG, and ensures her chest X‑ray is updated before surgery.

6. Packed RBCs of type O+ are prepared. After reviewing the chart, which findings should you report to the physician? Select all that apply.
	1. Hemoglobin: 14 g/dL, Hematocrit: 42%
	2. WBC count: 4,500/μL
	3. ABG: pH 7.40, pCO₂ 35 mmHg, HCO₃ 24 mEq/L
	4. Platelet count < 150,000/μL
	5. Platelet count > 450,000/μL
	6. Mrs. Pham has O– blood type
	7. Antibiotic prophylaxis scheduled 30 minutes before skin incision

<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>1, 3, 5</li>
	<li>4, 5, 6</li>
	<li>1, 3, 5, 6, 7</li>
	<li>1, 2, 3, 7</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>B. **4, 5, 6**. Eliminate options A, C, and D by determining that Item 1 is normal.

7. Mrs. Pham underwent ABG. Which of these should you not report to the surgeon?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>pH 7.50, pCO₂ 10, HCO₃ 22</li>
	<li>pH 7.35, pCO₂ 30, HCO₃ 22</li>
	<li>pH 7.40, pCO₂ 20, HCO₃ 22</li>
	<li>pH 7.20, pCO₂ 26, HCO₃ 22</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>B. **pH 7.35, pCO₂ 30, HCO₃ 22**. This is the least variance presented.

8. Mrs. Pham’s daughter, Dani, arrives in the ED with RUQ pain, distention, and vomiting; free air under diaphragm on X‑ray indicates perforated duodenal ulcer. Which pre‑op med should be administered?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Morphine</li>
	<li>Neomycin</li>
	<li>Propranolol</li>
	<li>Aspirin</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>B. **Neomycin**. Prophylactic antibiotics are used to reduce surgical risks.

9. In PACU, Mrs. Pham’s orders show: HR 120, BP 100/50, RR 30, Hgb 10, WBC 5,000, Platelets 300,000. Which nursing intervention should you implement?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Monitor for infection and request antibiotics</li>
	<li>Elevate the legs</li>
	<li>Encourage early ambulation and increase fluids</li>
	<li>Investigate bleeding and apply pressure</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>B. **Elevate the legs**. This is the modified trendelenburg position used for patients in shock (evidenced by hypotension, tachycardia, and tachypnea). Option D can be a follow-up action if the shock is hemorrhaging in nature.

10. Five days post‑laparotomy, Dani coughs forcefully and feels a sharp “let go” at the incision. Which interventions were done after she was rushed to the ED? Select all that apply.
	1. Notify the healthcare provider
	2. Open the dressing to view the problem
	3. Apply pressure over the site
	4. Use sterile dressing and saline to keep incision moist
	5. Sit client upright in bed

<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>2, 3</li>
	<li>1, 4</li>
	<li>1, 2, 4</li>
	<li>2, 3, 4, 5</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>C. **1, 2, 4**. The nurse immediately notifies the healthcare provider. To determine required management, the nurse may open the dressing to view the problem. Afterwards, sterile dressing and saline can be used to keep the incision moist.
>
>"Evisceration" refers to the exiting of organs through the incision. "Dehiscence" refers to the detachment of the wound.

>[!EXAMPLE] Situation 3
>Nurse Krea is working on a surgical ward and is responsible for ensuring informed consent is properly obtained.

11. A 45‑year‑old for appendectomy signed consent but says, “I didn't really understand what the doctor said.” What should the nurse do next?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Reassure and proceed with surgery prep</li>
	<li>Ask family to explain the surgery</li>
	<li>Delay and notify surgeon for clarification</li>
	<li>Explain the surgery herself</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>C. **Delay and notify surgeon for clarification**. Only the physician explains the procedure, eliminating options B and C. The informed consent is not valid, so option A is inappropriate.

12. A 17‑year‑old requires emergency splenectomy after an accident; parents haven’t arrived. What should happen regarding informed consent?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Wait for parents</li>
	<li>Ask the minor to sign</li>
	<li>Proceed under implied consent due to emergency</li>
	<li>Contact a lawyer</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>C. **Proceed under implied consent due to emergency**. This is used in life-threatening situations where valid consent cannot be applied.

13. In telephone consent, what is required for validity? Select all that apply.
	1. At least 1 nurse present
	2. At least 2 nurses present
	3. Thoroughly documented chart
	4. Ordering person must sign when present
	5. Signature within 2 days

<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>2, 3, 4</li>
	<li>2, 3, 5</li>
	<li>1, 3, 4, 5</li>
	<li>2, 3, 4, 5</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **2, 3, 4**. There must be at least two nurses present, with one serving as a witness. Documentation must be thorough, and a countersign must be placed as soon as possible or within 24 hours.

14. A Stage 4 cancer patient refuses chemotherapy consent, wishing to explore alternatives. What is the nurse’s most appropriate action?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Ask doctor to convince patient</li>
	<li>Respect decision and notify physician</li>
	<li>Have family sign instead</li>
	<li>Continue chemotherapy as ordered</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>B. **Respect decision and notify physician**. The patient has the right to refuse medical treatment.

15. Patient is unconscious and needs surgery consent. Arrange decision-makers in order:
	1. Patient
	2. Spouse
	3. Adult child
	4. Parents
	5. Legal guardian
	6. Adult siblings
	7. Social worker

<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>1, 4, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7</li>
	<li>4, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7</li>
	<li>2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7</li>
	<li>1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>C. **2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7**. The patient is unconscious, but otherwise it would be option D. This is the order of priority in obtaining informed consent.

>[!EXAMPLE] Situation 4
>Preoperative teaching educates patients before surgery to reduce anxiety, increase understanding, and promote recovery.

16. The nurse taught deep‐breathing and coughing exercises. Client still does which, indicating need for further teaching? Select all that apply.
	1. Sitting upright before breathing/coughing
	2. Taking deep breaths before coughing
	3. Placing hands vertically on either side of incision
	4. Using a pillow to splint during coughing
	5. Gentle coughs sounding like throat clearing

<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>3, 5</li>
	<li>1, 2, 4</li>
	<li>1, 2, 4, 5</li>
	<li>2, 3, 4, 5</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **3, 5**. The hands are placed directly over the splint. The patient should also clear the throat forcefully.

17. NPO status causes thirst and hunger. Nurse explains purpose of NPO status is:
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Easier anesthesia induction</li>
	<li>To avoid aspiration</li>
	<li>To prevent bleeding</li>
	<li>To improve wound healing</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>B. **To avoid aspiration**. Anesthesia during surgery can diminish reflexes that prevent aspiration.

18. Client asks why surgical history matters. Nurse responds previous surgeries can:
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Interfere with anesthetic absorption</li>
	<li>Affect comprehension</li>
	<li>Affect CNS</li>
	<li>Alter responses to surgery</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>D. **Alter responses to surgery**. This is an umbrella option. It is also unlikely that surgical history will affect anesthetic absorption and CNS status.

19. Nurse asking about vision/hearing; family asks why. Nurse replies primary reason is:
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>This will help us determine the need for additional resources after discharge</li>
	<li>This will help assess the risk of accidents in the home after surgery, which could affect the surgical outcome.</li>
	<li>This helps identify any unanticipated needs prior to beginning the surgery.</li>
	<li>This will help us to individualize how we provide preoperative and postoperative teaching.</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>D. **This will help us to individualize how we provide preoperative and postoperative teaching**. Options A and B focus only on the postoperative scenario, while option C only focuses on the preoperative scenario.

20. Client sees note “palliative” for nerve-root resection and asks what it means. Nurse explains:
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Surgery schedule is overbooked</li>
	<li>Surgeon objects to the surgery</li>
	<li>Exact procedure undecided</li>
	<li>Procedure relieves pain but won’t cure the problem</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>D. **Procedure relieves pain but won’t cure the problem**

>[!EXAMPLE] Situation 5
>Nurse Minji, an OR Nurse of NJZ Hospital, assesses multiple clients that are scheduled for surgery. Currently, there are 5 patients Nurse Minji needs to assess and each of these patients raises a concern. As a professional nurse, Nurse Minji must provide accurate, fact-based information to ease their concern.

21. Nurse Minji started her shift by checking Haerin, a 3-year-old child who has not had surgery before, who is being prepared for tympanoplasty. Haerin’s mother expresses concern about the child’s psychological adaptation to surgery. While planning for postoperative care, the nurse recognizes that the child is likely to have which greatest concern based on age?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Anticipated pain</li>
	<li>Body image changes</li>
	<li>Communication difficulties</li>
	<li>Clinginess from parents</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>D. **Clinginess from parents**. Separation anxiety is particularly strong in a toddler (3-year-old patient). Eliminate options A and C as they are physiological and social adaptations respectively.

22. Nurse Minji goes to the second room for assessment of Teodoro, a 71-year-old client, scheduled for colon cancer surgery. Nurse Minji should know that one of an increased surgical risk of Teodoro is:
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Potential fluid and electrolyte imbalances related to decreased kidney function</li>
	<li>Hyperacidity as postoperative complications related to increased hunger sensation</li>
	<li>Noncompliance related to inability to comprehend the seriousness of surgical interventions, leading to noncompliance</li>
	<li>Decreased pain sensation related to poor cardiovascular status</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **Potential fluid and electrolyte imbalances related to decreased kidney function**.

23. The following clients are in the preoperative holding area. Minji is tasked to determine which client will be undergoing the most serious or major surgery for her to be able to watch out for any signs of complications and intervene as soon as possible if such complication occurs.
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Jisoo, scheduled for tonsillectomy</li>
	<li>Lisa, who will be undergoing biopsy of the breast</li>
	<li>Jennie, who will be undergoing arthroscopy after a knee injury</li>
	<li>Rosé, scheduled for nephrectomy to donate it to her friend</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>D. **Rosé, scheduled for nephrectomy to donate it to her friend**. The kidneys are a major organ.

24. A client who takes numerous medications is being prepared for surgery. The nurse reviewing the client medication list is most concerned about which medication that increases surgical risk?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Amiodarone</li>
	<li>Lorazepam</li>
	<li>Hydrocortisone</li>
	<li>Metformin</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>C. **Hydrocortisone**. Hydrocortisone is a corticosteroid and therefore depresses the immune system. This increases surgical risk, especially for infection.

25. When reading the chart of the patients in the nursing station, Nurse Minji should know that which among the following patients is most likely to be at higher overall surgical risk?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Adie, who has dementia</li>
	<li>Niki, who is culturally different than the medical personnel</li>
	<li>Maki, client who has mild anxiety</li>
	<li>Earl, a client who has had previous surgeries</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **Adie, who has dementia**. Dementia is a major barrier to postoperative teaching and cooperation as it alters memory, cognition, and social abilities.

>[!EXAMPLE] Situation 6
>Minji, Dani, and Hanni are all OR nurses of NJZ Hospital. It is a toxic day for them since there are multiple surgeries scheduled in their shift where they would scrub in. After Nurse Minji assessed the patients during their preoperative phase, their patients are now undergoing surgery.

26. It was Haerin’s operation today when Nurse Hanni, the circulating nurse, notices that the surgeon's gown has touched the surgical light, but she is unsure of it because she was dizzy and tired due to her 24-hour shift. What is the best action Nurse Hanni should do?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Inform the surgeon and assist in re-gowning and re-gloving</li>
	<li>Replace the sterile drape over the contaminated sleeve</li>
	<li>Cover the contaminated area with a sterile towel</li>
	<li>Do nothing as the rest of the gown remains sterile</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **Inform the surgeon and assist in re-gowning and re-gloving**. If in doubt, consider it unsterile.

27. Hyein, the surgeon, has been irritated by Nurse Hanni’s callout during the operation. She reprimanded Nurse Hanni because it delayed the procedure which might cause an increased risk of complication for the patient. As the surgery continues, Hyein continuously asks Nurse Hanni about scenarios where sterile technique is compromised. Among the following statements, Hyein will reprimand Nurse Hanni if she says?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>I can touch the instruments in the sterile area</li>
	<li>Objects that are out of my range of vision are considered as unsterile</li>
	<li>I can lower my hands below the level of my waist</li>
	<li>If I saw Nurse Dani, the scrub nurse, had accidentally been in contact with a wet surface, I will call her out to scrub out</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **I can touch the instruments in the sterile area**. The nurse in question is the circulating nurse; an unsterile member. They are not able to interact with the sterile field.

28. In preparation for the next surgery, Nurse Minji helps in setting up the operating room. Which of the following should Nurse Minji not do in order to prevent contamination?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Pouring sterile solution into a sterile basin from a height of 6 inches</li>
	<li>Turning back to the sterile field while adjusting the equipment</li>
	<li>Discarding a sterile pack after noticing a small tear in the wrapper</li>
	<li>Maintaining a one-foot distance from the sterile field if unsterile</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>B. **Turning back to the sterile field while adjusting the equipment**. The back is considered unsterile.

29. Nurse Dani asks her senior OR nurse, Minji, what is the purpose of 15 air exchanges per hour in a standard operating room? Nurse Minji, with 5 years of experience being an OR nurse, is correct if she states that it is to:
<ol class="mcq-options">

	<li>Decrease airborne bacteria shed by staff</li>
	<li>Facilitate airflow for temperature regulation</li>
	<li>Allow gas anesthesia to dissipate rapidly</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>B. **Decrease airborne bacteria shed by staff**. The remaining options are inaccurate.

30. While preparing instruments for Teodoro’s surgery, Nurse Minji notes that a sterile package has a small amount of moisture on the outer wrapper. What should Nurse Minji do given the situation?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Use the instrument as long as the inner wrapper is dry</li>
	<li>Dry the wrapper with a sterile towel and continue use</li>
	<li>Consider the item contaminated and replace it</li>

>[!EXAMPLE] Situation 7
>After assisting in several procedures throughout the shift, Nurse Dani and Nurse Hanni proceeds to the Post-Anesthesia Care Unit (PACU) to follow up on the recovery of the five surgical patients she previously assessed. She closely monitors each patient's vital signs, level of consciousness, surgical site, and pain level.

31. Teodoro, 71 years old, with Diabetes Mellitus is now admitted to the RR after a colon cancer surgery. Nurse Minji should do what appropriate nursing intervention to prevent common complication related to this case?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Encourage Teodoro to perform leg exercises and ambulate as early as possible</li>
	<li>Apply warm compresses to the surgical site twice daily</li>
	<li>Limit fluid intake to prevent abdominal distention</li>
	<li>Keep the legs elevated with pillows placed directly under the knees</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **Encourage Teodoro to perform leg exercises and ambulate as early as possible**. Patients with DM is at higher risk for deep vein thrombosis from viscous blood and micro-/macrovascular changes.

32. While planning postoperative care for Earl, an obese client prior to surgery, the Nurse Dani should be most concerned about which risk during the postsurgical recovery period?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Impaired wound healing</li>
	<li>Fluid overload</li>
	<li>Pressure ulcer development</li>
	<li>Inability to regulate body temperature</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **Impaired wound healing**. Decreased blood flow as a result of obesity and fat deposits in blood vessels will result in impaired wound healing.

33. Nurse Hanni is implementing measures to prevent thrombophlebitis. Which measure should be the priority action of Nurse Hanni?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Apply prescribed sequential compression devices.</li>
	<li>Reinforce the importance of smoking cessation.</li>
	<li>Assess the legs with each set of vital signs.</li>
	<li>Teach the client to report Homans sign.</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **Apply prescribed sequential compression devices**. These devices alternate pressures on the legs to enhance venous return.

34. Nurse Dani assesses the wound of a Maki to have moderate drainage with a greenish tinge. The nurse should take which priority action next?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Document the expected findings</li>
	<li>Check for bleeding at the base of the wound</li>
	<li>Take the pulse and blood pressure, and compare with previous readings.</li>
	<li>Note the latest temperature and white blood cell count</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>D. **Note the latest temperature and white blood cell count**. The nurse will suspect infection from the drainage characteristics and evaluate temperature and white blood cell count.

35. Nurse Hanni and Nurse Dani are teaching Maki about wound care in preparation for discharge. How should Nurse Hanni and Nurse Dani evaluate the effectiveness of home care teaching on wound care?
	1. Give a paper-and-pencil quiz
	2. Have the caregiver or client demonstrate the procedure
	3. Have the client or caregiver explain the procedure
	4. Have the client or caregiver critique a video on the procedure
	5. Ask the client detailed questions while demonstrating the procedure

<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>2, 3</li>
	<li>1, 2, 5</li>
	<li>2, 3, 5</li>
	<li>1, 2, 3, 4, 5</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **2, 3**. The most effective method for evaluating teaching is a return demonstration. Options 1 and 4 are possible, but not as effective. Option 5 can inhibit learning by distracting the patient.

>[!EXAMPLE] Situation 8
>Anesthesia is the use of medications to prevent pain and discomfort during surgical procedures. It allows the patient to undergo surgery safely, comfortably, and without awareness of the procedure. The type of anesthesia used depends on the surgical site, procedure type, patient condition, and preferences of the surgical team.

36. A patient is scheduled for an open abdominal surgery under general anesthesia. Which of the following statements best describes general anesthesia?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>It involves injecting a local anesthetic near a nerve to block sensation in a specific region</li>
	<li>It provides sedation and pain relief while allowing the patient to remain conscious</li>
	<li>It induces a reversible loss of consciousness, amnesia, analgesia, and muscle relaxation</li>
	<li>It is used only for minor outpatient procedures</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>C. **It induces a reversible loss of consciousness, amnesia, analgesia, and muscle relaxation**.

37. Nurse Alex is monitoring a patient undergoing surgery with general anesthesia. He is aware of the critical need to watch for metabolic side effects. Which of the following is considered the most dangerous metabolic side effect that could arise during the procedure?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Hypothermia</li>
	<li>Hyperglycemia</li>
	<li>Hypotension</li>
	<li>Hyperthermia</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>D. **Hyperthermia**. Malignant hyperthermia is a hyper-reactive process to anesthesia that is present in some individuals that causes continuous skeletal muscle contractions and producing excess body heat. The antidote is a muscle relaxant, **Dantolene**.

38. Nurse Anna receives a patient in the postanesthesia care unit (PACU) following a procedure with general anesthesia. What is the most crucial assessment Nurse Anna should focus on?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Monitoring respiratory status</li>
	<li>Evaluating pain levels</li>
	<li>Checking level ofonsciousness</li>
	<li>Measuring vital signs</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **Monitoring respiratory status**. This is a common response to anesthetic agents. Vital signs is relevant, but is less specific than option A.

39. Nurse Kelly is assessing a patient for early signs of local anesthetic toxicity. She is aware of the typical symptoms to watch for. Which one of the following is not an early sign of local anesthetic toxicity?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Hypertension</li>
	<li>Tinnitus</li>
	<li>Numbness around mouth</li>
	<li>Lightheadedness</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>A. **Hypertension**. Options B, C, and D are CNS manifestations.

40. Which of the following differentiates epidural anesthesia from spinal anesthesia?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Epidural anesthesia is injected into the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), while spinal anesthesia is injected into the epidural space.</li>
	<li>Epidural anesthesia provides continuous pain relief, while spinal anesthesia is typically a one-time injection.</li>
	<li>Spinal anesthesia is commonly used in labor and delivery, while epidural anesthesia is used only for major surgeries.</li>
	<li>Epidural anesthesia causes total unconsciousness, while spinal anesthesia does not.</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
