>Nurse Mary Grace Piattos is conducting a health education class in a local barangay. She explains the nature of diseases and infections to help families understand how illnesses develop and spread. She asks the participants a few questions to test their understanding.

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>B. **Eliminating the susceptible host**.
>
>>[!INFO] Chain of Infection
>>1. **Causative Agent**: the pathogens responsible for causing disease
>>2. **Reservoir**: the location in which the causative agent multiplies, i.e., the habitat.
>>3. **Portal of Exit**: the medium that allows the causative agents to leave the reservoir.
>>4. **Mode of Transmission**: the transportation method that allows causative agents to spread. It is the weakest link in the chain of infection.
>>5. **Portal of Entry**: an entry point on a potential host that the causative agent uses to infect the host.
>>6. **Susceptible Host**: a host that is unable to ward off the causative agent from infection and colonization. This is related to age, sex, lifestyle, genetic makeup, and immune status.

<ol class="mcq-options">

	<li>Susceptible host</li>
</ol>

>[!EXAMPLE] Situation 8
>Mr. Angelo, 27, visits the Rural Health Unit for a check-up. He mentions a painless sore on his penis that appeared 2 weeks ago but healed on its own. He had unprotected sex with a new partner a month ago. Nurse Carla recommends a VDRL test, which comes back reactive for syphilis.

36. Mr. Angelo: “I don't need to get tested for syphilis because I don’t see any wounds or rashes.” Nurse:
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>“Syphilis always shows symptoms early.”</li>
	<li>“You're right—if you have no symptoms, you're safe.”</li>
	<li>“Syphilis can be present even without visible symptoms.”</li>
	<li>“Testing is only needed if you feel sick.”</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
>C. **"Syphilis can be present even without visible symptoms."**
>
>>[!TIP] Syphilis
>>
>>Syphilis is also known as *Bad Blood*, *Lue's Venereal Disease*, and *The Great Imitator* (as it mimics other diseases).
>>- **Causative agent**: *Treponema pallidum*
>>- **Mode of transmission**: sexual contact, fomites, needles, vertical transmission
>>- **Incubation period**: 19 to 90 days
>>- **Stages**:
>>		1. Primary stage: the appearances of chancres—painless sores.
>>		2. Secondary stage: a non-pruritic reddish-pinkish rash, and condyloma lata/condyloma acuminata (warts) appear on the body.
>>		3. Latent stage: asymptomatic stage
>>		4. Tertiary stage: the spread of syphilis into other body organs and tissues—neurosyphilis, cardiosyphilis, gummasyphilis.

37. Mr. Angelo: I was treated for syphilis before. It is enough to build immunity against syphilis, right? The nurse appropriately responds:
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>“Yes, you're permanently immune after that treatment.”</li>
	<li>“That's correct, you are 100% safe.”</li>
	<li>“You can get syphilis again if you are exposed to it.”</li>
	<li>“Don't worry, only women can get reinfected."</li>
</ol>
