>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
Neonates are born with low levels of vitamin K because it does not readily cross the placenta, and their livers are immature. Option 1 is incorrect because the issue is not with the fetal blood cells themselves but with the clotting factors. 96. If parents keep a toddler dependent in areas where he is capable of using skills, the toddler will develop a sense of which of the following?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>Mistrust</li>
	<li>Shame</li>
	<li>Guilt</li>
	<li>Inferiority</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
According to Erikson's stages, toddlers (ages 1-3) are in the Autonomy vs. Shame and Doubt stage. If parents overly control or do not allow toddlers to practice skills they are capable of performing (thus keeping them dependent), the toddler may develop feelings of shame and doubt about their own abilities. Mistrust is from infancy, guilt from the preschool stage, and inferiority from school age.

100. Which of the following suggestions should the nurse offer the parents of a 4-year-old boy who resists going to bed at night?
<ol class="mcq-options">
	<li>"Allow him to fall asleep in your room, then move him to his own bed."</li>
	<li>"Tell him that you will lock him in his room if he gets out of bed one more time."</li>
	<li>"Encourage active play at bedtime to tire him out so he will fall asleep faster."</li>
	<li>"Read him a story and allow him to play quietly in his bed until he falls asleep."</li>
</ol>

>[!FAQ]- Rationalization
Establishing a calming bedtime routine is essential for helping children wind down and fall asleep. Reading a story and allowing quiet play in bed (Option 4) provides a transition from active play to rest and can help the child relax. Allowing them to fall asleep in the parent's room (Option 1) can create dependency. Threatening to lock the child in (Option 2) is punitive and fear-inducing. Encouraging active play at bedtime (Option 3) is counterproductive, as it stimulates rather than calms.
