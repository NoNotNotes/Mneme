# Individuals
**Individuals** are the **entry point** of the other levels of clientele. The focus of care is usually on the person's health or medical problem. There are two approaches to caring for the individual:
1. **Atomistic Approach**: the client is perceived by its components and parts.
2. **Holistic Approach**: the client is perceived by its whole; its totality. This is the approach utilized by the nurse.

>[!INFO] Community-Based Nursing
>The community health nurse does not focus solely on individuals. Rather, they focus on the community and well population. Nurses who operate in the community for individuals (or families), who are usually sick, are referred to as *community-based nurses* (CBN). CBNs deliver secondary and tertiary prevention practices often found in the hospital in the community.
>
>In contrast, a community health nurse focuses on communities (or families) who are generally well, providing promotive and primary preventive services.

# Family

>[!CITE] Also read
>[[10-family-assessment]] (wip)
>
>[[274a-family-health-nursing]]

A **family**, from a systems perspective, is defined as **a collection of people who are integrated, interactive, and interdependent**. Each member affects the other. The family as a whole is responsible for the health of all members. In the family level, the nurse provides care to two or more individuals in a single household. Because most people belong to a family and their health is strongly influenced by the home situation, **the family is considered the basic unit of care in community health nursing**. A family may be defined as a group of people who are related by blood, birth, legality, or simply by choice.

The family unit is important to society. It meets individual needs, and supports spouses or partners by meeting affective, sexual, and socioeconomic needs. The family is also viewed as an important unit of health care with awareness that **the individual can be best understood within the social context of the family**. Observing and inquiring about family interaction enables the nurse in the community to assess the influence of family members on each other. Specifically, reasons to work with families are enumerated by Friedman et al.:
- "**The family is a critical resource**": the family can improve individual members' health through health promotion and wellness activities.
- "**In a family unit, any dysfunction that affects one or more family members will affect the members and unit as a whole**": this is also referred to as a "*ripple effect*".
- "**Case finding**": while assessing an individual, the nurse may identify a health problem that necessitates identifying risks for the entire family.
- "**Improving nursing care**": the nurse provides better and more holistic care by understanding the family and its members.
## Functions of the Family
There are six functions of the family%% (*mn. SHARES*)%%:
1. **Socialization of Family Members**: the family creates the foundation for how its children becomes productive members of society. It involves the transmission of culture from the parents to their children.
2. **Healthcare**/**Physical Maintenance**: the provision of basic needs for its dependent members.
3. **Affection**/**Welfare and Protection**: the family provides companionship and meets affective, sexual, socioeconomic needs, and emotional gratification. The family is a source of motivation and morale for its members.
4. **Reproduction**/**Procreation**: the universally accepted institution for reproductive function and child rearing.
5. **Economic Function**: the family serves as a basic unit of economic production (rural families producing goods) or economic consumption (urban families earning salaries or wages)
6. **Status Placement**: the family confers its societal rank on the children. With social mobility, the family may shift between social classes.

The following are additional forms and classifications discussed:
1. Based on **Authority**, where decision-making is delegated to either the father (**Patriarchal**) or mother (**Matriarchal**). A participative form of authority is termed **Patricentric**, **Matricentric** or **Egalitarian** (equal), eliminating the hierarchal nuance of previous terminology.
2. Based on **Marital Residence Pattern**, where the family moves closer to or at the father's residence (**Patrilocal**), moves closer to or at the mother's residence (**Matrilocal**), or independent of either spouse's location (**Neo-Local**)
3. Based on **Lineage**; the system of descent and inheritance where lineage, family name, and property are passed down the male line (**Patrilineal**) or the female line (**Matrilineal**)
4. Based on **Amount of Partners**, a family may be **Monogamous**, where one husband is bound to one wife; **Polygamous**, where a husband is with multiple wives (commonly found among Muslims in Saudi Arabia); or **Polyandrous**, where a wife is with multiple husbands (commonly found in the Todas of Southern India).
## Duvall's Family Development Theory
Also known as the **Family Life Cycle** and **Stages of the Family Cycle**, Evelyn Duvall (1988) theorized the way families move through stages in a particular order across time after members successfully master tasks for each stage.

| **Name/Description**         | **Age of Oldest Child**   | **Developmental Task**                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| I: **Beginning families**    | No children               | Adjusting to married life                                   |
| II: **Childbearing**         | 0–30 months               | Adjusting to infant care                                    |
| III: **Preschool children**  | 2½–5 years                | Socializing and guiding                                     |
| IV: **School-age children**  | 6–13 years                | Encouraging school achievement                              |
| V: **Families with teens**   | 13–20 years               | Balancing independence & responsibility                     |
| VI: **Launching families**   | Young adults leaving home | Transition to adult‑to‑adult relationships                  |
| VII: **Middle-aged parents** | Empty nest to retirement  | Refocusing on couple & aging parents                        |
| VIII: **Aging families**     | Retirement to end of life | Coping with retirement, losses, and end‑of‑life transitions |

The basis for which stage a family is the highest stage reached, i.e., **the eldest child**. For example, a family with two children—one newborn and one twenty-year-old—is considered to be in Stage V.

If all children are over 21 years old and still living with their parents, Duvall considers the family **abnormal**, considering it as a developmental fixation on the fifth stage of the family cycle.

# Population Groups (Aggregates)
**Population groups** refer to people who share common **characteristics**, **developmental stage**, and **common exposure to common health problems**. Some of these groups are children, women, farmers, factory workers, and elderly. The nurse responds to **the needs of a group or aggregate**, while maintaining recognition of each individual's situation and circumstance. Salient groups to consider are those that are at risk, including infants and children, pregnant women, adolescents, and the elderly. This section briefly touches on public health concerns and programs tackling them.
## Infants
**Malnutrition** is one of the largest concerns in infants in the Philippines.
- **Vitamin A Deficiency** results in xerophthalmia, which produces xerosis (excessively dried and wrinkled cornea), then ulceration, then scarring, then blindness. This is marked by a pathognomonic sign of **Bitot's spot**, a triangular white-grayish spot on the sclera produced by wrinkling. It also produces **night blindness**.
- **Iron Deficiency**, which produces anemia.
- **Iodine Deficiency** can result in goiter and mental retardation.

**DOH Programs** are in place to counteract the most common types of nutritional deficiencies in the Philippines:
- **Micronutrient Supplementation Program** targets children 5-year-old children and below in the *Garantisadong Pambata* program performed at April and October.
- **Food Fortification Act** (R.A. 8976) aims to fortify commonly eaten foods such as junk foods and other foods with essential nutrients (marked by the *Sangkap Pinoy* Seal) to reduce rates of deficiency.
- **Kalusugan at Nutrisyon ng Magnanay Act** (R.A. 11148), also known as the *First 1000 Days Law*, outlines the nutritional guidelines of a child's first 1000 days, starting from pregnancy up to 2 years of age.

>[!INFO] **Pneumonia** is the most common cause of infant and child death in the Philippines.

## Pregnant Women
The greatest to **maternal health** complications of pregnancy, one of which is hypertension (PIH, preeclampsia, eclampsia). Globally, the greatest contributor is postpartum hemorrhage.
- The community health nurse has the responsibility to refer at-risk pregnancies to the proper facilities; the focus of the community health nurse is the *normal pregnant woman*. She watches out for danger signs%% (*mn. ABC*)%%:
	- Abdominal pain and aches (headache, body aches)
	- Blood pressure, blood glucose, bleeding (in any trimester)
	- Convulsions, chills/fever (most commonly due to UTI)

The **Four Pillars of Safe Motherhood** program outlines proper prenatal care, facility-based care (BEmONC standards), clean and safe delivery, and proper postpartal care.
### Prenatal Care
Prenatal Care is monitored via the Home Based Maternal Record (HBMR), more recently a Maternity Booklet. Visits should be performed at least 4 times: 1st trimester, 2nd trimester, 3rd trimester, and at least once on the 8th month onward. However, ideally, visits should be made every two weeks from the eighth month onward.
- If the mother fails to complete the four minimum prenatal visits, **the mother is no longer permitted to deliver in a lying-in clinic** due to heightened risk. They will breferred to a hospital.
- **Maternal supplementation**:
	- **Vitamin A**: 4th month of pregnancy, 10,000 IUs twice a week.
	- **Iron**: 4th month of pregnancy, 60 mg/cap od for 6 months equaling 180 caps. For anemia mothers, the dose is doubled to 120 mg/cap.
	- **Folate**: Even before pregnancy, 400 mcg to reduce neural tube defects (spina bifida)
	- **Iodine** is optional and is given once per pregnancy, 200 mg.
- **Health Teaching** is performed about the normal biologic changes in pregnancy
### BEmONC Facilities
Basic Emergency Obstetrics and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) Facilities are found in Barangay Health Stations, Rural Health Units, Lying-in Clinics, and Birthing Centers should all be certified as BEmONC-capable facilities.
- To qualify as a BEmONC facility, there must be **at least one RN or one RM**, and an **on-call physician**.
- The WHO recommends that there be at least **one BEmONC facility for every 125,000 population**.
- Nurses in BEmONC facilities have been given six emergency interventions for mothers:
	1. Parenteral administration of **oxytocin**.
	2. Parenteral administration of **anticonvulsant**.
	3. Parenteral administration of an initial dose of **antibiotics**.
	4. **Assisted deliveries** in the absence of a physician.
	5. **Removal of retained products of conception**.
	6. **Manual removal of retained placenta**.
- Nurses in BEmONC facilities have been given three emergency interventions for neonates:
	1. **Newborn resuscitation**
	2. **Treatment of neonatal sepsis**
	3. **Oxygen** support
### Clean and Safe Delivery
All deliveries must be **facility-based**. Home deliveries are no longer permitted as stated in the **DOH AO No. 29 s. 2008**, the "*No Home Birthing Policy*". This offset **hilot** workers and traditional birthing attendants, but they may still be permitted to participate in **assisted deliveries**.

### Postpartal Care
At least two postpartal visits are performed. The first is done within the first week, preferably **3 to 5 days post-delivery**, or within 72 hours according to Famorca. The second postpartal visit is done by the sixth week, when the postpartum period ends.
1. **Maternal Supplementation**:
	1. **Vitamin A**: the mother is given 200,000 IUs of Vitamin A within 4 weeks postpartum.
	2. **Iron** 60 mg/cap and **folate** 400 mcg daily for three months (90 caps)
2. **Postpartum assessment**%% (mn. *SHE-BUBBLE*)%%:
	- **Skin Turgor**
	- **Homan's Sign**: a sign of DVT; pain at the calf upon dorsiflexion of the foot.
	- **Episiotomy/Episiorrhaphy**: check for signs of infection.
	- **Breast Changes**: lactation and changes in the breast occurs postpartum:
		- Nipple inversion, potentially resulting in altered body image. Sucking by the significant other, nipple rolling, and suction via syringe or breast pumps may allow the nipple to be everted.
	- **Uterus**: assess for shape (normally pear-shaped) and consistency (firm).
	- **Bowel**: the reappearance of flatus and bowel sounds show the absence of paralytic ileus.
	- **Bladder**: assess for retention or incontinence.
	- **Lochia**:
		- **Lochia Rubra**: brown to red.
		- **Lochia Serosa**: red to pink.
		- **Lochia Alba**: pink to white.
		- Also assess for other discharges: yellow/greenish (infection), gray (gonorrhea), and frothy/cheesy (candidiasis)
	- **Emotional Changes**: postpartal blues, postpartal depression, and postpartal psychosis.
## Adolescents
Issues common in adolescents span a large range of activities and conditions due to their growing independence and ability. This includes violence, substance abuse (alcohol, tobacco, illicit drugs), malnutrition, and sexual health risks (sexually transmitted diseases, teen-age pregnancy).
1. The **Adolescent and Youth Health Development** (AYHD) **Program** targets the 10 to 19 year old demographic. R.A. 10354, the **Reproductive Health and Responsible Parenthood Law**.
## Elderly
The **Elderly**, older adults aged 65 years old and above, makes up 4% of the population (potentially reflecting high fertility rates and a lower life expectancy). Semantically, those referred to as "*senior citizens*" are individuals 60 years old and above.
- **Functional age** is age based on remaining physical activity rather than biological age. In elderly clients within the community, the nurse bases their assessment on the *functional age* of the elder, i.e., their ability to perform activities of daily living.
- The **projected life expectancy** of Filipinos averages **75 years old** for women and **70 years old** for men.
- The most common cause for mortality in the elderly is **ischemic heart disease**, followed by **cancer**.

# Community
**Community**: the **primary and entire client** of the community health nurse.
1. **Elements** of the community:
	- Social Element: the communications and interactions of people.
	- Geographical Element: the physical boundaries of the community.
	- Psychocultural Element: norms, values, and beliefs of the people.
	- Political Element: governance and leadership of the people.
2. **Types** of community: The Philippine is now more urbanized than ruralized. The priority type of community of the DOH are **geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas** (GIDAs), where healthcare is still inaccessible.
	- Rural Community: less populous, often more agricultural.
	- Urban Community: more populous, often more industrial.
	- Rurban Community: a combination of rural and urban community types.
	- Suburban Community: the periphery of an urban community.
	- Metropolitan Community: actively expanding urban areas.
3. **Classifications** of communities can be identified by:
	- **Geopolitical boundaries**, the traditional method, outlines communities based on their scope of political territory, such as barangays, purok, municipals, cities, states, regions, provinces, and republic.
	- **Phenomenological division**—interest-based division e.g., the LGBTQIA+ community, or communities of various professions.
