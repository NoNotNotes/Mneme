# Assessment
Procuration of data, either **primary** (sourced from the community) or **secondary** (sourced from records). There are five steps in assessment:

## Data Collection
**Data collection** may be performed to obtain **primary data** (interview, observation, surveys, questionnaires, community forums or assemblies, and focus group) or **secondary data** (review of records and reports)
1. **Interview**: most reliable, "best method".
	- **Informant interview**: the traditional interviewee-interviewer set-up
	- **Key person interview**: "key people" are the primary data source, such as leaders of the barangay (chairman, captain), teachers, priests, and the elderly. Notably, members of the healthcare team are not considered key persons.
2. **Observation**:
	- *Participant Observation* involves participating in the community and groups being observed.
	- *Rapid Observation*, the type of observation used during *ocular inspections*, also known as "*windshield observation*."
3. **Surveys and Questionnaires** makes up the majority of data collected.
4. **Community forum** or **assembly**, an open meeting for the community with many participants.
5. **Focus group**, a smaller meeting of approximately 6 to 12 participants. Notably, this differs from focus group discussions as it is a method of assessment rather than of intervention.
6. **Review of Records and Reports**: a source of secondary data that focuses on the documents of the target population. This includes records from [[15-census|census]], [[16-vital-statistics|vital statistics]], [[14-fhsis|FHSIS]], [[18-nesss|NESSS]], etc.
	- **Registration**
		- **P.D. 651**: registration must be completed within 60 days
		- **P.D. 766**, the more recent law on registration, amends two sections from P.D. 651: section 2, which discusses birth registrations, and section 5, which discusses death registrations.
			- Section 2: Birth registration must be done within 30 days, and is free of charge. Births are either facility-based or non-facility-based. Facilities may be hospital-based or lying-in. For facilities, reports are handled by the physician, nurse, or midwife. In outside births, a report can be made by anyone who has knowledge of the birth, e.g., the husband, for which the birth registry will be created by the local health officer. Registrations are done at the local civil registrar.
			- Section 5: Death registration must be done within 30 days, and is free of charge. Deaths are either hospital-based or non-hospital based. Deaths occurring in a hospital is reported by the last attending physician who attended to the patient or who pronounced the individual as dead. If the death occurred outside, anyone who has knowledge of the death may report the death, for which the death registry will be created by the local health officer, or else the mayor, or else the municipal secretary. Both reports from the physicians or witness must be reported to the local civil registrar within 48 hours. Death reports are registered where the death occurred. Deaths should be registered at the same location of their registration.
		- **P.D. 856**: the Sanitation Code of the Philippines states that a death certificate required prior to burial. This is because a death registration determines the cause of death. Individuals with highly infectious diseases are not permitted a burial, but rather a cremation.
	- **Field Health Service Information System**: according to E.O. 352, the FHSIS is the official recording and reporting system of the DOH.
		- Components of FHSIS:
			1. **Individual Treatment Record** (ITR; "Treatment Record") is the fundamental building block of FHSIS.
			2. **Target Client List** (TCL) is the secondary building block of the FHSIS. It curates lists of people based on services being rendered:
				- Prenatal or postpartal care, sick children, children under 1 year old, family planning, communicable disease, tuberculosis, etc.
			3. **Summary Table** ("**Tally Report**") summarizes the amount of health programs being utilized and morbidity.
			4. **Monthly Consolidation Table**
		- Reporting
			1. **Monthly Report**:
				- In the BHS, reported by the midwife.
				- M1: monthly program report
				- M2: monthly morbidity
			2. **Quarterly Report**:
				- In the RHU (done by nurse), Provincial Health Office (done by FHSIS coordinator), and Regional Health Office (done by coordinator)
				- Q1 (quarterly program report) is a three-month report based on M1.
				- Q2 (quarterly morbidity report) is a three-month report based on M2. Each facility sends reports to its higher level facility.
			3. **Annual Report**:
				- In all facilities, annual reports are produced as an output.
				- A-Brgy Report is performed by the midwife.
				- A1 Report outlines demography: births and deaths.
				- A2 Report outlines annual morbidity presented by age and sex.
				- A3 Report outlines annual mortality presented by age and sex.
	- **National Epidemiology Sentinel Surveillance System** is a hospital-based information of disease occurrence, also providing supplemental information for the DOH.
		- Data points:
			- Cases by geographical location
			- Demographic characteristics of the disease
			- Information
			- Estimates
			- Trends
		- Laboratory-diagnosed diseases under surveillance (mn. MaTCH):
			- Malaria
			- Typhoid Fever
			- Cholera
			- Coronavirus
			- Hepatitis A, B
			- HIV/AIDS
	- **Vital Statistics**, also known as **biostatistics** or **health statistics**, describe the population based on health indicators such as births, morbidity, mortality, fertility, dependence, etc.
		- Crude Birth Rate and Death Rate: Births/Deaths by mid-year population per 1000.
		- Maternal, Infant, Neonatal, and Fetal Mortality Rate: Deaths by live births per 1000.
		- Specific Death Rates: Deaths in sec. group/Applicable cases per 100,000.
		- Case Fatality Rate: same as SDR but per 100 (i.e., in percentage)
		- Morbidity: Cases by population per 100

## Data Collation
Categorization of data to facilitate ease of presentation.

## Data Presentation
- **Descriptive presentation**: presentation of data by narrative.
- **Numerical presentation**: presentation of data with charts and figures.
	- Line graphs: used to visualize trends over time
	- Bar graphs: used for comparison of variables
	- Pie chart: used for presentation of distribution or composition of at most 6 variables, with excess variables being collected into an "others" category.
	- Scatter diagram: used for correlation of variables
	- Population pyramid: visualization of the population by age and sex. This is often used to show, generally, the age and changes in the population.
		- Expansive type: high fertility and lower aged populations.
		- Constrictive type: lower aged and newborn populations.
		- Stationary type: a stable population outlined by fairly even distributions of population across the lifespan.

## Data Analysis
Comparison of data to standards and norms.
## Data Utilization
Use of findings to improve health of the facility.
## Family Assessment
1. **First Level of Assessment**: finding the family health problems with the **initial database** (IDB), also known as the *family health assessment form*. The desired output of this level of assessment is the **typology of nursing problem**: health deficits, health threats, and foreseeable crises.
	- **Health deficits** are problems that already exist; a "*you*" problem.
	- **Health threats** are problems found in the environment; an "*external*" problem.
	- **Foreseeable crises** are family situations that could result in health problems.
2. **Second Level of Assessment**: the performance of the family health task, and the recognition of the problem (*salience*). The output of this level of assessment are statements of **inability** of the family to promote and maintain health.
	- **NANDA** has a set of diagnoses for families that may be used by the nurse.
	- The **Family Coping Index** by Maglaya is another diagnostic tool for the family.
## Community Assessment
Also known as *Community Situational Analysis* or *Community Diagnosis*. This may be performed in two methods:
1. **Comprehensive Community Diagnosis**: a general assessment of the community to determine the presence of problems.
2. **Problem-Oriented Community Diagnosis**: assessment conducted to address a known problem, such as in the event of an outbreak.

The **Omaha system** is used as the tool of assessment for the community. It delineates community problems into various domains:
1. **Environmental domain**
2. **Psychosocial domain**
3. **Physiological domain**
4. **Health-related Behaviors**: nutrition, hygiene, family planning, sleep and rest, exercise

The output of community assessment are the **community health problems**. This includes the following:
1. **Health status problems**: actual diseases in the community
2. **Health resource problems**: problems with services, manpower, programs, etc. that result in impaired health status.
3. **Health related problems**: health-related behaviors mentioned earlier.

# Community Planning
1. **Prioritization**: discussed below.
2. **Goal Setting**: Be SMART--specific, measurable, attainable, realistic, and time-bound
3. **Plan Formulation**
	1. Creation of the **Family Nursing Care Plan**
	2. Creation of the **Community Health Plan** (CHP) by the nurse
4. Setting **Evaluation Parameters** to be used once intervention has been performed.
## Prioritization
### Family Problem Scaling
1. **Nature of the Problem**: first level assessment of the problem
2. **Modifiability**: whether or not the problem can be affected or changed; the most important (highest weight) criterion.
3. **Preventive Potential**: the likelihood of the problem being prevented from occurring in the future
4. **Salience**: the family's perspective on the problem.

| Criteria                  | Score | Weight | Result |
| ------------------------- | ----- | ------ | ------ |
| **Nature of the Problem** |       | 1      |        |
| - Health deficit          | 2     |        | 1      |
| - Health threat           | 1     |        | 0.5    |
| - Foreseeable problem     | 0     |        | 0      |
| **Modifiability**         |       | 2      |        |
| - Easily modifiable       | 2     |        | 2      |
| - Partially modifiable    | 1     |        | 1      |
| - Non-modifiable          | 0     |        | 0      |
| **Preventive Potential**  |       | 1      |        |
| - Highly                  | 2     |        | 1      |
| - Moderate                | 1     |        | 0.5    |
| - Low                     | 0     |        | 0      |
| **Salience**              |       | 1      |        |
| - Needs to be addressed   | 2     |        | 1      |
| - Problem, but no action  | 1     |        | 0.5    |
| - No problem              | 0     |        | 0      |

# Community Implementation
Health care plans for the community are put into action during this phase. Interventions are classified according to the parties involved:
1. **Independent**: the community performs interventions by themselves.
2. **Interdependent**: the community collaborates with the nurse to perform interventions.
3. **Dependent**: the community depends on the nurse to perform interventions, or the nurse performs interventions in stead of the community.

The community nurse focuses on the independent and interdependent interventions, while the clinical nurse focuses on interdependent and dependent interventions.

# Community Evaluation
Evaluation can be performed in either a **qualitative** or **quantitative** method. While the community health nurse focuses on qualitative evaluation, quantitative approaches are still important for comprehensive evaluation. The components of evaluation are:

| Component                |                      | Parameters                   |
| ------------------------ | -------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Structure Evaluation** | Inputs and resources | Adequacy and appropriateness |
| **Process Evaluation**   | Procedures performed | Effectiveness, efficienc     |
| **Outcome Evaluation**   | Results              | Met, Partially Met, Unmet    |
